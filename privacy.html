This policy applies to the game “Cloud Scrubbers” (shown on the device as “Cloud Scrub”) for Android and for iPhone and iPad (package and bundle ID com.dannylabs.cloudscrubbers).

2. In short

  • Cloud Scrubbers has no user accounts and no server of its own. Your progress stays on your device (and, if applicable, in your own backup with Google or Apple, see section 7).
  • The game shows video ads only when you choose to watch one (for example, for double Suds or a third star). So that a video is ready, the game loads one in advance through Google AdMob.
  • The game uses Google Analytics for Firebase to measure how it is played (for example, at which levels many players give up) so we can improve it. Only numbers and fixed words are sent, never names or free text. In the EEA, the UK, and Switzerland only with your consent; everywhere you can turn it off.
  • Purchases are handled by Google Play or the App Store. We never see payment details.
  • You can change your choices in the game at any time: Settings → Privacy (consent) and Settings → Game statistics (statistics).

3. Consent

On launch, where the law of your country requires it (for example in the EU, the EEA, the UK, and Switzerland), the game asks for your consent before a video is loaded or any statistics are sent. The form comes from Google’s “User Messaging Platform”. You can accept, decline, or choose individual purposes. Your choice is stored on the device until you delete the app, and you can change or withdraw it at any time under Settings → Privacy. A new choice applies right away. In countries without this requirement, the same row opens this policy.

Independently of that, you can turn game statistics off and on again in every country under Settings → Game statistics.

4. Ads (Google AdMob)

Provider: Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland.

The game never shows ads on its own, only videos that you choose and that give you a reward. So that a video is ready, the game loads one in advance through Google AdMob after launch (and after every video shown). Even while loading, Google processes, among other things:

  • your device’s advertising ID for ads, frequency capping, reporting, and fraud prevention, also for non-personalized ads (in the EEA, the UK, and Switzerland only with your consent; on Android only if you have not deleted it; on iPhone and iPad only if you allow it in Apple’s tracking prompt – see below),
  • a device identifier that applies only to this app or to apps from the same developer (on Android the app set ID, on iPhone and iPad the identifier for vendors), also without consent, for example for frequency capping and fraud prevention,
  • the IP address,
  • information about the device and the app (model, operating system, language),
  • your approximate location, estimated from the IP address,
  • information on whether and how the video was shown.
  • With consent, Google may use the advertising ID; whether the videos are tailored to your interests depends on the purposes you agree to.
  • Without consent (where it is required), Google shows “limited” ads: not personalized, without the advertising ID, and with less data. A video cannot be delivered with no data processing at all, among other things for fraud prevention.

The game tells Google that it is not directed at children and limits the videos to content suitable for teens.

Google is an independent controller for the ads (Google’s controller-controller terms). Legal basis: for personalized ads and the use of the advertising ID, your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); for limited ads, our legitimate interest in funding the free game (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). How long Google keeps ad data is governed by Google’s policies: https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads and https://policies.google.com/privacy

On iPhone and iPad, after a short explanation, the game also uses Apple’s tracking prompt (“App Tracking Transparency”) to ask whether it may use the advertising ID (IDFA). iOS releases it only if you allow it there. Without your permission, Google does not receive it, and ads are not tailored based on your activity in other apps and on websites. You can change your choice at any time in the iOS settings (Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking).

On Android, you can reset or delete the advertising ID in the settings (Settings → Google → Ads).

5. Game statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

Provider: Google Ireland Limited (see above).

Statistics are switched off when the app starts. The game only switches them on when this is allowed:

  • in the EEA, the UK, and Switzerland only if you consent (to the purposes “Store and/or access information on a device” and “Measure content performance”);
  • in other countries as long as you do not turn game statistics off (Settings → Game statistics).

These five game events are sent, with numbers and fixed words only:

  • Level start (level_start): level number, world, which attempt at this level, type (level or Daily Cloud).
  • Level end (level_end): level number, world, outcome (won, lost to the storm, or quit), stars, seconds played, how often play continued after the storm. If the app is closed in the middle of a level, the game reports “quit” (or “lost to the storm” if the storm screen was open) for that level once at the next launch, provided statistics are allowed then.
  • Game tip (tutorial_step): which tip was shown for the first time.
  • Video offered (video_offer): which video slot was offered (for example “third star”), level number.
  • Video watched (video_watched): which video played to the end and gave its reward, level number.

With the Video Pass, no video events are sent.

In addition, a random ID of the app installation, device type, operating system, language, approximate country, usage time, and events such as first launch, session start, and app update are collected automatically. For purchases through Google Play and the App Store, Firebase may also record product, price, and currency automatically. We do not use the advertising ID for statistics; on Android, statistics do not collect the Android ID either, and on iPhone and iPad not the identifier for vendors (IDFV). The game does not release the statistics for advertising.

Here Google acts as a processor on our behalf (Google’s data processing terms). The legal basis is your consent in the EEA, the UK, and Switzerland (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), and otherwise our legitimate interest in a good game (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), which you can object to at any time with the switch.

We use the data only to improve the game (difficulty, tips, offers). It is not sold. Google deletes data that can be linked to a device after at most 14 months; aggregated reports without a link to a device are kept.

If game statistics are off or your consent is missing, nothing is sent. A level you leave while statistics are off is not reported later either.

6. Purchases (Google Play and App Store)

Purchases in the game go through Google Play on Android and through Apple’s App Store on iPhone and iPad. Google or Apple processes your payment and account data under its own privacy policy. The game only receives a confirmation of which product was bought and a purchase ID (legal basis: contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). On Android, the game checks the receipt on the device; no receipt is sent to us. The game stores the purchase ID in your progress so that no purchase is credited twice.

7. Progress

Your progress (levels, stars, Suds, workshop, wardrobe, album, pass, purchased items) and your settings (language, music, sounds, vibration, game statistics) are stored on your device. Next to them, the game notes there how often you started a level and which level is currently open, for the information in section 5. If you use Android’s backup, Android may copy this data to your own Google backup so it comes back after you switch devices or reinstall; you control this in Android’s settings. On iPhone and iPad, it is part of your iCloud or computer backup if you use one; you control this in the iOS settings. We never receive it.

8. Transfers abroad

Google may also transfer data to countries outside Switzerland and the EEA, in particular to the USA. For this, Google relies on the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses and on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and the Swiss-US Data Privacy Framework.

9. Children

Cloud Scrubbers is intended for teens aged 16 and older and for adults. It is not directed at children.

10. Your rights

Under the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) and, where applicable, the GDPR, you have the right to:

  • access,
  • rectification,
  • erasure,
  • restriction of processing,
  • objection,
  • data portability,
  • withdrawal of consent (under Settings → Privacy or Settings → Game statistics).

Because the game has no accounts, we usually cannot find you in the data held by Google ourselves. Write to us at dannylabs.studio@gmail.com and we will help as best we can. You can also contact a supervisory authority: in Switzerland the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC), in the EU the authority of your country of residence.

11. Changes

If the game changes so that other data is processed, we will update this policy. The version published here applies.
